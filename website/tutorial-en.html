1 · What playing Trioio means

Trioio is not played note by note. It is a conversation in four moves:

  1. You bring a recording: the reference.
  2. Trioio listens to it and composes a response: a new file, entirely synthesised, that never contains the reference's audio.
  3. You listen and judge: the whole response, one section or a stretch.
  4. Trioio learns from your judgements. The next response changes because of your listening.

Playing means choosing the reference, the mode, the language and what counts. The instrument composes; you lead by listening.

Tip: Leaving every choice as it is is also a choice: Trioio then decides from what it hears.

2 · Bring and authorise a recording

  1. Click "choose a reference": it takes you to the Library.
  2. Click "bring a recording…" and pick the file.
  3. A new recording starts as pending. Click "authorise…" and state its origin (your own, a capture, another instrument, AI, licensed, public domain…), with what right it may be used and who declares it.
  4. Click "authorise".

You can also drag one audio or MIDI file into the window. In the Library, "source audio ▾" shows the registered file or keeps a copy in Trioio's data folder under fontes/. "library folder ▾" opens the current folder or lets you choose another one.

Without authorisation Trioio does not compose on a recording. This protects provenance: every response keeps the record of where its reference came from.

If the app opens in demonstration mode, use "library folder ▾" to choose where Trioio keeps its data.

Tip: Start with a short recording of your own, 30 s to 2 min: the first listening is quicker and you know the material.

3 · Listen to the reference

On the "Listening and response" page, click "▶ reference".

The first analysis takes a little while; the status line says Trioio is listening. When it is done, the REFERENCE strip shows the sections Trioio read: where the material changes, the pulse, the key and the language it recognised.

This reading is what the response will talk to. It is kept in the data folder, so the next time it is instant.

4 · Choose the mode

The mode is the attitude of the response:

• follow: it goes along with the reference, same field, same pulse;
• transfigure: the same matter, another figure;
• oppose: it goes against it, another register, another density;
• interrupt: it cuts in, enters and leaves.

The four buttons sit at the top of the window. The mode also decides how a style read in the reference is answered: follow plays in that style, transfigure goes to a neighbour, oppose to its contrary.

Tip: Compose the same reference in follow and then in oppose: the difference between the two teaches more than any description.

5 · Compose

Click "compose again".

While Trioio works, a bar at the bottom of the window shows each stage (listening, reading the choices, the network when it is on, the score, the voices one by one, the master, saving) and the time spent. A response takes from a few seconds to about a minute, depending on its length and on the ensemble.

The response appears in the RESPONSE strip. Each new response is a new version: nothing is overwritten, the earlier ones stay in "Your listening".

6 · Listen, and the Third Eye

• ▶ plays the response.
• "▶ together" plays the reference and the response at the same time, for listening only.
• The master sets the volume of everything.
• "file ▾", next to the response: show its files in the folder, export them (WAV, MIDI, stems) to wherever you choose, or move the response to the trash (it can be brought back).

The Third Eye is Trioio's unpredictable part: the gestures, the deviations, the choices that do not come from the reference. It stays hidden until you have listened, so it does not steer your ear. Then click "reveal after listening" to see what it did and where.

The amount of unpredictability is set in the Language panel.

Tip: Listen once without revealing, judge, and only then reveal: you will learn what the Eye does by ear before reading it.

7 · Judge

First choose what you judge:

• click outside the selection: the whole response;
• click a block of the RESPONSE strip: that section;
• drag across the RESPONSE strip: the marked stretch.

Write a note if you like: your sentence about that listening, such as "the chip sound got too hard". It is kept with the judgement and shown in "Your listening".

Then click accept, transform or reject. The judgement weighs on the next responses, and on the rules of the game: accepted rules stay in memory, rejected ones go to the compost.

Tip: Judging a stretch is more useful than judging everything: Trioio learns which choice worked where.

8 · Compare versions

On the "Your listening" page:

  1. Click a version in the list: it becomes A.
  2. Click another: it becomes the new A, and the previous one becomes B. Green and blue badges show which is which.
  3. Use "▶ compare A × B" and "switch A ↔ B". Switching jumps to the other version at the same moment of the music.

To the right you see what your listening taught (how much each choice rose or fell with your judgements), the rules of the game in version A, and the box "the next response takes the listening into account".

9 · The Language panel

"Language ▸" opens the choices. All of them are optional, and each one cites its source in the form of the response.

• Mixture: scales to combine, by name or by family, separated by + or commas ("rāga Yaman + maqam Hijaz", "Greek modes, African scales").
• Pitches, technique, seam, cycle, voicing, intention, form, tempo.
• Ensemble: the instruments of each voice by style (choro, samba, jazz, forró, tango, rock, orchestra…), or read from the reference.
• Harmony: the language of the harmony machine.
• Unpredictability (the Third Eye) and the modular tear (from the instrument to the unheard).
• Dialogue (answer in the pauses), game (Trioio invents its own rules), film effects, the sound of the aura, stems.

Tip: Change one choice at a time between two responses: that way you hear what each one does.

10 · Words, influences and gesture

Words: write a few words about the response you want, in any language ("dançante, maracatu, virada", "cold rain, slow"). Trioio reads intention, form, dance and the nodes of the influence tree, and shows in the form what it understood and what it did not. Left empty, Trioio follows its own criteria. Accents, capitals, punctuation and small typing mistakes are tolerated.

The box "look up unread words online" sends only the words Trioio did not read to public services (Wikidata, Datamuse) and calculates from their answers. It is off by default, because the words leave the computer; answers are remembered, so the second time is instant.

Influences: nodes of the tree (rock/noise, brasil/nordeste/maracatu) or auto; the strength goes from the reference (0) to the tree (1). Without: roles to leave out (percussion, bass).

Gesture: draw the intensity curve of the piece, or write it as position:intensity.

Artists and bands: Trioio does not bring the poetics of artists and bands; it does not invent how someone composes. A name written in Words only reaches the network as loose words. To make an artist an influence, write it yourself, with your reading and your sources, in the file influencias_do_autor.txt in the data folder (Trioio creates it with a commented example). The line "também:" gives the names as you write them, for example "também: júpiter maçã"; from then on the name calls that node in Words and in Influences.

Tip: The influences are tendencies, never rules: "rock" makes drums likely, not compulsory. Mix two distant nodes to get somewhere new.

11 · A first exercise

  1. Use a short recording of your own.
  2. Compose in follow. Listen, judge the whole response.
  3. Compose again in oppose.
  4. In "Your listening", compare the two with "switch A ↔ B".
  5. Turn on the game in "Language ▸" and compose again.
  6. Read the rules in "Your listening". Judge by stretch where a rule worked well or badly.
  7. Compose two or three more times. The responses should start to change with what you accepted and rejected.

To take a response to a DAW, turn on "stems" in the Language panel: next to the mix, Trioio saves one WAV per voice.
